# Character / Subject Style — "The AI Race No One Can Afford to Win"

Companion to `visual_style_bible.md`. This film is people-and-institutions-centric, not AI-centric — the storyboard's own cross-cutting note is explicit: "No humanoid robots, evil-AI imagery, robot faces, glowing brains, code rain, or generic AI stock-footage cliches anywhere in the storyboard." This file governs how the *human* figures who do appear should be depicted.

## Core rule

There is no AI character, robot character, or personified-AI figure anywhere in this film. AI is depicted exclusively through its *effects and infrastructure* — dashboards, data centers, documents, institutions — never through a **humanoid robot**, an **evil AI face**, a glowing brain, or any other anthropomorphized-machine visual. This is a hard rule, not a stylistic preference: the script's own thesis (scene_031, scene_032) explicitly rejects the "rogue machine" framing in favor of "a set of decisions, made under competitive pressure, by people" — so a humanoid-AI visual would directly contradict the narration.

This rule was already enforced once during footage retrieval: `footage/retrieval_report.md` records that an initial scene_032 candidate was rejected specifically for being a humanoid-robot image ("Editorial correction ... scene_032 initial humanoid-robot candidate rejected ... Orphan robot MP4 left untouched"). Treat that as the concrete precedent for how strictly this rule is enforced project-wide.

## Human figures — depiction rules

- **Realism level:** photorealistic (real footage) or, if ever needed for an AI-fallback still, photoreal-adjacent illustration matched to the base palette — never cartoon, never stylized/vector figures, never uncanny-valley synthetic faces.
- **Anonymity:** every human figure in the storyboard is generic and unbranded — researchers, analysts, regulators, executives, office workers. No scene identifies a specific real named individual. Depict people as representative professionals (business/lab-casual attire consistent with the institutional setting), never as caricatures or stock-photo clichés (no exaggerated "eureka" poses, no stock "hacker in a hoodie" trope).
- **Framing of people:** favor backs-of-heads, silhouettes, over-the-shoulder, and hands-on-documents/keyboards framing over identifiable close-up faces, especially in sensitive scenes (boardroom in scene_007, evaluation room in scene_004–005) — this keeps the film from implying it has identified specific real individuals or companies.
- **Emotional register:** calm, procedural, observational — professionals doing their jobs, not visibly panicked, triumphant, or menacing. Even tense scenes (scene_007's boardroom, scene_009's benchmark-overtake beat) stay at "serious concern," not melodrama.
- **Diversity:** depict a realistic range of ages/genders/backgrounds across the generic researcher/analyst/regulator figures used throughout — avoid defaulting to a single demographic for "AI lab employee" or "executive."

## Scene-specific notes

- **scene_006 (fire-drill metaphor):** people evacuating calmly, walking (not running), consistent with the storyboard's explicit note — this is a literal visual match for the narration's own metaphor, and panic would misread the metaphor as a real emergency.
- **scene_031/scene_032 (closing sequence):** quiet human silhouettes in office windows — deliberately anonymous and still, reinforcing "decisions made by people," not a single hero or villain figure. No machine, no robot, no personified AI anywhere in this closing visual — human presence only.
- **scene_027 (safeguards/auditors):** collaborative, unhurried group review — the warmest, most human-positive framing in the film, marking the pivot from critique to constructive proposal.

## Institutional/organizational depiction

- Companies and governments are represented by their generic physical infrastructure (anonymous office towers, data-center campuses, government facades, legislative chambers) — never by a logo, a named sign, or a recognizable real building presented as a specific named institution, per the storyboard's per-scene factual-sensitivity notes (scene_001, scene_007, scene_019, scene_020).
- No scene should visually suggest a factual claim about a specific named company or government beyond what `scripts/script.md` states in its hedged, "reportedly"/"disclosed"-qualified language.
