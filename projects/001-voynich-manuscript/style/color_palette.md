# Color Palette — The Voynich Manuscript

A muted sepia + deep teal duotone system. Every image should read as if
these five colors are the only colors available — no saturated modern hues
(no bright red, neon, or pure white) anywhere in the video.

| Name | Approx. hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Aged Parchment Cream | `#E8DCC0` | Base light tone — vellum pages, paper texture, light backgrounds |
| Faded Ink Umber | `#5C4632` | Primary dark/mid tone — ink strokes, wood, leather bindings, shadows on skin/fabric |
| Deep Archive Teal | `#1F3B3B` | Secondary dark tone — vault interiors, night/gap-in-history scenes, cool shadow fill |
| Candlelight Amber | `#C98A3B` | Warm accent — flame light, highlights on faces and gold leaf, focal-point glow |
| Shadow Charcoal | `#221F1E` | Deepest shadow / vignette edges, never pure black |
| Antique Gold (accent only) | `#B08D3D` | Reserved for on-screen text, thin dividing lines, and small emphasis details — never a large fill area |

## Usage rules

- Every scene should be dominated by Cream, Umber, and Teal; Amber appears as
  a light source or focal accent, not a fill color; Gold is reserved for
  graphic-style elements only (see `graphic_style.md`), not for illustration
  content itself.
- Desaturate everything else that appears — greenery in the "biological
  section" scenes should read as a muted teal-green consistent with Deep
  Archive Teal, not a bright natural green.
- No pure white and no pure black anywhere — use Cream and Shadow Charcoal as
  the palette's lightest and darkest points instead, to keep the aged,
  non-digital feel.
- Lighting direction is warm-to-cool: a single warm (Amber) key light source
  per scene (candle, lamp, or lab spotlight) against cooler Teal/Charcoal
  ambient shadow — this is the core "cinematic lighting" contrast referenced
  in the project's style brief.
