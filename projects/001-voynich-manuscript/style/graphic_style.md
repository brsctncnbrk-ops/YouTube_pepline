# Graphic Style — The Voynich Manuscript

Covers on-screen text, dividers, and diagram/data overlays — the layer that
sits on top of the painterly illustrations.

## On-screen text

- Rendered as if **engraved or letterpress-printed**: a serif typeface with
  slightly worn, uneven edges (not a crisp modern sans-serif), in Antique
  Gold (`#B08D3D`) or Aged Parchment Cream, depending on what reads clearest
  against the background.
- Text sits inside a **restrained plaque or lower-third band** — a
  translucent Shadow Charcoal or Deep Archive Teal panel with a thin gold
  hairline border — never a bare drop-shadowed text floating with no
  containment.
- Keep text short (a title, a date range, a fact tag) — this is a
  documentary accent, not an infographic slide. No bullet lists or paragraph
  blocks on screen.

## Dividers and small graphic elements

- Thin Antique Gold hairlines (single pixel-weight equivalent, not bold
  bars) used sparingly to separate a text plaque from the frame edge or to
  underline a title card.
- Any icon or symbol (e.g. a star marker referencing the manuscript's own
  margin stars) should be rendered in the same etching/woodcut linework
  style as the rest of the illustration — never a flat modern icon-font
  glyph.

## Diagram / data overlays (scenes 10-12: statistical evidence, cipher,
radiocarbon dating)

- Data visualizations (word-frequency graphs, clustering diagrams, cipher
  grids, date-range graphics) must be **stylized as archival diagrams**, not
  modern dashboard charts: hand-drawn-looking lines, parchment-textured
  background, Umber/Teal ink tones instead of clean vector lines, no bright
  chart colors (no red/green/blue data series — use tonal variations within
  the palette only, e.g. distinguishing two data series by Umber vs. Teal
  line weight, not by hue).
- These overlays should look like they could plausibly sit inside the
  manuscript itself, or in a researcher's annotated notebook — reinforcing
  the archival mystery tone rather than breaking it with a modern-looking
  infographic.

## What to avoid

- No sans-serif, no drop shadows on bare text, no bright saturated chart
  colors, no flat/modern icon sets, no full-screen text blocks.
