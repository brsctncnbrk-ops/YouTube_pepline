Somewhere in a climate-controlled vault at Yale sits a book that has defeated every attempt to read it for over 600 years.

The Voynich Manuscript (Beinecke MS 408) is a radiocarbon-dated medieval codex (1404-1438) filled with botanical, astronomical, and pharmaceutical illustrations, and a flowing script that matches no known language on Earth. We walk through what's actually in its pages, who owned it (including Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II, who paid 600 gold ducats for it), and the WWII codebreakers who spent decades trying, and failing, to crack it.
